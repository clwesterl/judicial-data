Median of the Majority

For each term, the justice who most often held the median position inside the winning coalition — and where that justice sat on the Judicial Common Space scale. Not the Court median, which the grey line tracks for comparison: the two coincide only when the Court is unanimous.

How the median is found. Within each formally decided merits case, the justices who voted with the majority (majority == 2) are ranked by their JCS score for that term; the middle justice takes one credit, or the two middle justices take half each when the coalition is even-sized. A term's label is whichever justice collected the most credit that term, and the point is plotted at that justice's own score in that term.

What the sample leaves out. 9,401 of 27,714 formally decided merits cases: a case drops out when any member of its winning coalition has no JCS score for that term, which also ends the series at OT2022, where JCS justice-term scores stop. Attrition is heaviest in the earliest terms, so the 1937–40 points rest on thin data.
